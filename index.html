📡 What is AdSense?
Google AdSense — Explained
Before estimating revenue, understand the product you're estimating.
📰

What is AdSense?

AdSense is a free, simple way for website publishers to earn money by displaying ads next to their online content. Google places targeted ads — relevant to content or user searches — on the publisher's pages.

⚙️

How Does It Work?

Publisher adds code to their site → Google displays targeted ads → Visitors click ads → Publisher earns money. Google AdSense works on a cost-per-click and revenue-sharing basis. Publishers get a share of the advertiser bid (Traffic Acquisition Cost — TAC).

🎯

Scope of This Estimate

Third-party websites only — NOT Google properties like YouTube or Search. We estimate the entire AdSense gross revenue including the publisher's TAC share, for one year.

📖 Terminology
CPC, CPM & CTR — The Three Core Metrics
CPC

Cost Per Click

Advertisers pay per click on their ad. If CPC = $0.10 and users click 1,000 times → advertiser billed $100.

Lower CPC achieved by: Higher Quality Score, better ad relevance, strong landing page experience
CPM

Cost Per Mille (1,000 Impressions)

Advertisers pay per 1,000 ad views — regardless of whether users click. Primary alternative to CPC for impression-based campaigns.

Cost per impression = CPM ÷ 1,000
CTR

Click-Through Rate

% of ad impressions that result in a click. Connects impressions to clicks in the revenue calculation.

CTR = Clicks ÷ Impressions × 100%

🌳 Steps 2–4 — Build the Tree
Assumptions & Estimation Tree

📐 The Master Formula

Ad Revenue = # Publishers × Daily Revenue per Publisher × 365 days

🎯 The 20/80 Rule — Pareto Principle

Based on general knowledge: the top 100 online publishers range from The New York Times (~10M daily subscribers) to niche publishers like Popular Mechanics (~10,000 monthly readers).

⚡
The key assumption: 20% of the top publishers are likely generating 80% of AdSense revenue. We estimate for the top 20 out of 100 publishers and scale up at the end — so our estimate represents 80% of the total.
🌍 Pareto Everywhere The 20/80 rule appears constantly in product analytics: 20% of features drive 80% of usage; 20% of customers generate 80% of revenue; 20% of publishers generate 80% of AdSense revenue. Using this signals to the interviewer that you think in distributions, not flat averages.

🔢 Step 5 — Bottom-Up
Assigning Numbers & Calculating
Aditi uses herself as the proxy "average NYT reader."

🖱️ Click Ads — Leaf Nodes

Pages visited / day= 10
Ads per page= 5
% that are click ads= 50% (0.5)
Click ads seen / visitor / day= 10 × 5 × 0.5 = 25
Source: Typical NYT reader — ~30 min morning + breaks, ~10 articles/day; ~5 ads/page observed.

👁️ Impression Ads — Leaf Nodes

Pages visited / day= 10
Ads per page= 5
% that are impression ads= 50% (0.5)
Impression ads seen / visitor / day= 10 × 5 × 0.5 = 25
Same as click ads — the other 50% of the 50 ads seen per day.

🖱️ Click Ad Revenue / Publisher / Day

# Visitors/day (avg across top 20)= 5,000,000
Click ads seen per visitor= 25
CTR (common digital display avg)= 0.5% (0.005)
CPC (common average)= $0.50
Click ad revenue / publisher / day= 5M × 25 × 0.005 × $0.50 = $312,500 ≈ $0.3M
Range 1M–10M, no other data point → average = 5M visitors/publisher.

👁️ Impression Ad Revenue / Publisher / Day

# Visitors/day= 5,000,000
Impression ads seen per visitor= 25
CPM (avg display)= $1.00
Cost per impression (CPM ÷ 1000)= $0.001
Impression revenue / publisher / day= 5M × 25 × $0.001 = $125,000 ≈ $0.13M

📊 Final Rollup to Annual Revenue

Click ads revenue / publisher / day= $0.30M
Impression ads revenue / publisher / day= $0.13M
Total ad revenue / publisher / day= $0.43M ≈ $0.4M
Top 20 publishers × $0.4M × 365 days= $2.92B
This $2.92B = 80% of total (Pareto rule)÷ 0.8
Total AdSense Revenue (100%)≈ $3.6 Billion / year

🔍 Finding the Error — Subscriber vs. Total Traffic

Our estimate of $3.6B is off by a factor of ~4.7 (17 ÷ 3.6). What went wrong?

⚠️
Root Cause: We assumed daily visitors = subscribers only. In reality, the majority of people who visit these publishers are non-paying visitors — they read without subscribing.

The Correction:

Non-subscriber : subscriber ratio ≈ 3:1 → For every 1 subscriber, 3 non-paying visitors read the same content. Total traffic = 4× subscriber count.

Old: 5M visitors/day  →  Revised: 5M × 4 = 20M visitors/day
# REVISED CALCULATION WITH 20M VISITORS

Click ads: 20M × 25 × 0.005 × $0.50 = $1.25M/day/publisher
Impression ads: 20M × 25 × $0.001 = $0.50M/day/publisher
Total: $1.75M/day/publisher

20 publishers × $1.75M × 365 days = $12.8B
$12.8B ÷ 0.8 (Pareto scaling) = ~$16B

# Actual (Alphabet 10K): $17B → Error: <6% ✅
🌍 Why Sanity Check Matters to BCG BCG, McKinsey, and Bain interviewers often give partial or full marks to candidates who: (1) Arrive at a wrong number AND catch it in the sanity check, (2) Correctly identify which assumption drove the error, (3) Demonstrate they can self-correct in real time. Aditi's revised $16B vs. the actual $17B is a <6% miss — excellent for a back-of-envelope estimate with no data.
🧠 Quiz — Sanity Check & Assumption Correction
Your guesstimate of AdSense revenue is $3.6B, but the reported figure is $17B. Which single assumption change would bring your estimate closest to the actual value?
✅ Correct! Answer: C
The discrepancy is ~4.7×. Since visitors appear in BOTH click and impression revenue formulas, a 4× increase in visitors multiplies total revenue by 4×, reaching ~$14–16B. Options A and D affect only one revenue stream and the multiplier would need to be much higher to reach $17B alone. Option B contradicts the 20/80 rule.

💡 Key Takeaway

A guesstimate isn't about being right the first time — it's about catching and explaining your errors. Self-correction in the sanity check is what impresses BCG interviewers most.
